### **Why this changes the competitive strategy**

With ProviderTrust, Quickflows might try to differentiate through AI-led process execution layered on verified data.

With symplr, Quickflows might emphasize easier implementation and reduced manual operations without requiring a full platform replacement.

With Medallion, those two messages are no longer sufficient. Medallion also offers AI-assisted workflows, platform integrations, managed services and evidence-backed efficiency claims.

To win against it, Quickflows will need a more specific, measurable advantage in verification accuracy, cost, workflow execution, implementation effort, source coverage or customer service.

### **3.2 CVO Credentialing — core direct competition**

This is Medallion's primary-source verification and credentialing service.

Its workflow covers:

1. Provider data intake and document gathering.  
2. Verification from primary sources such as licensing boards, FSMB, and other credentialing sources.  
3. Automated construction of credentialing packets.  
4. Identification and resolution of missing or inconsistent information.  
5. Expert review and credentialing quality assurance.  
6. Delivery of a file prepared for committee review.  
7. Recredentialing and related ongoing oversight.

Medallion advertises 55 million completed primary-source verifications, an average of one day for credentialing file readiness, and processing 3.5 times faster than legacy CVOs on average.

Medallion

Crucial distinction: One-day file readiness does not mean every professional is fully credentialed, granted clinical privileges, approved by every health plan, and ready to bill within one day.

Those are separate operational milestones with different external dependencies.

### **3.3 Provider Enrollment — converting credentialing into revenue**

This is one of Medallion's strongest commercial differentiators.

After a clinician is credentialed, they often still need to be enrolled or rostered with relevant payers before their services can be billed as in-network.

Medallion offers two main approaches:

| Enrollment path | How it works | Business impact |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Direct enrollment | Agents help prepare, submit, track, and follow up on enrollment with Medicare, Medicaid, and commercial payers | Reduces application errors, administrative follow-up and delays |
| Fast Track Network | Uses existing payer relationships and a network-specific credentialing/rostering arrangement where available | Can shorten the time to billable status for eligible payers |

Medallion reports approximately 75,000 payer enrollments completed over the preceding 12 months and a median 57 days from submission to payer decision. That demonstrates why its Fast Track message is commercially significant: it attempts to reduce dependence on individual payer queues where existing relationships permit it.

Medallion

For Quickflows, this represents both a potential product expansion and a constraint. AI can automate a significant portion of enrollment work, but it cannot simply eliminate payer policies, external approval requirements, or the time needed to establish network agreements.

### **3.4 Roster Management**

A healthcare organization may need to send different provider information to different payers, using different templates and data requirements.

Medallion's roster product supports standardizing data, detecting discrepancies, monitoring add/termination changes and preparing submissions.

It also checks issues such as NPI mismatches and inconsistent provider roles. The site reports about 500,000 provider records managed in this workflow context.

Medallion

This is strategically adjacent to Quickflows' verification service, but it is a separate operational process.

### **3.5 Delegated Credentialing**

Delegation allows a qualifying organization to carry out credentialing on behalf of a payer under an approved agreement and oversight model.

Medallion helps customers prepare policies, manage verification, create committee-ready records, submit rosters, and support delegation audits.

Its website reports 99.9% of recredentialing completed before deadline and estimated administrative-cost reductions of 66%. These are company-reported figures and need a defined measurement population before comparison.

Medallion

The commercial value is much larger than a verification check: delegation can remove repeated payer-level credentialing delays and reduce redundant administrative processes.

### **3.6 Cross-State Licensing**

Medallion manages applications, source documentation, renewal schedules and state-specific requirements for clinicians who practice in multiple jurisdictions.

It advertises a median 63-day licensing timeline from request to board issuance and 18 days saved on average through compact licensing. The company cannot control when a state licensing board issues a license, so those figures need to be interpreted as observed workflow outcomes, not guaranteed government processing times.

Medallion

### **3.7 Ongoing Monitoring**

This is a particularly direct overlap with Quickflows and ProviderTrust.

Medallion's public monitoring coverage includes OIG, SAM, NPDB, Medicare Opt-Out, state licenses, DEA/CSR, Medicaid exclusions, OFAC, CMS preclusion and other eligibility sources.

It provides alerts, outcome classifications and verification history. Its help center confirms that certain license renewal checks occur weekly near expiration, while other monitoring services follow their relevant source and service logic.

Medallion+2

We should not accept a marketing phrase like continuous monitoring as proof of an identical refresh interval for every source.

### **3.8 Privileging**

Credentialing verifies qualifications; privileging grants authorization to perform specified clinical activities within a healthcare facility.

Medallion also supports the application, committee and approval workflows associated with privileging. This overlaps with symplr's broader provider lifecycle position, although their complete functionality and clinical content libraries would need deeper comparison.

Medallion

### **3.9 Payer Contract Management**

This is a newer strategic extension of Medallion's provider-operations proposition.

It uses AI to extract contract terms, effective dates, filing limits and other provisions, and helps organizations interpret fee schedules and implied reimbursement rates. It also supports payer-level contract rollups and negotiation preparation.

Medallion

This connects to Quickflows' broader Contract Compliance Agent concept. However, automating contract analysis is different from owning payer-contract negotiation or guaranteeing higher reimbursement.

### **3.10 CredAlliance — the network opportunity**

CredAlliance is Medallion's payer-facing credentialing-clearinghouse model, launched in August 2025\.

Instead of each payer independently duplicating verification work for the same provider, the model aims to reuse a compliant verification process across participating payer networks.

Medallion+1

This could become a significant long-term competitive moat. If a provider's credentialing file can be reused across multiple paying organizations under appropriate agreements, each new participant can improve the economic value of the network.

The number of participating payers, breadth of reuse, contractual rights and actual savings are not fully disclosed.

### **5.3 What this means for Quickflows pricing**

A basic per-verification comparison would miss much of Medallion's contract.

$Totalannualcustomercost=Platform+Verification+Enrollment+Monitoring+Services+Sourcefees$

Plus the customer's remaining internal labor, which is essential when calculating total cost of ownership.

For example, consider a hypothetical organization with 5,000 providers, 2,000 new enrollment events per year, and recurring compliance monitoring:

| Monitoring | 5,000 × \$1/month | \$60,000 |
| Other services | Assumed annual amount | \$30,000 |

This is a scenario for building a Quickflows economic model—not an estimate of Medallion's actual prices, contract value, or revenue. Source fees and customer's internal labor are excluded.

## **6\. Reverse-engineering Medallion's sales motion**

The likely enterprise sales process can be reconstructed from the website's conversion design, customer case studies, pricing model, and published customer experiences.

1\. Attract a problem-aware buyer

Credentialing delays, unbillable providers, licensing complexity, high administrative costs

2\. Demonstrate the financial consequence

Lost revenue, staffing costs, delayed growth, denial exposure and compliance risks

3\. Convert through a meeting or personalized demonstration

Evaluate provider volume, payer requirements, workflow complexity and existing systems

4\. Design a customized commercial engagement

Select the platform, credentialing, enrollment, licensing, monitoring and service components

5\. Onboard and operationalize

Import provider data, configure systems, connect sources, establish workflows and support teams

6\. Demonstrate results and expand

Improve turnaround, add providers, introduce additional modules and build deeper integrations

### **8.4 An important technical discrepancy to verify**

The current marketing page advertises webhook-based, real-time status updates.

These statements are inconsistent on their face.

| Metric | Required measurement |

Data completeness, source coverage, identity matching, continuous monitoring and evidence.

Workflow breadth, interoperability, service delivery, implementation and enterprise procurement.

Automation quality, specialist effort, turnaround, revenue impact and cost per completed outcome.

The eventual competitive advantage should be a measured operating outcome—not simply that Quickflows is AI-powered or less expensive.
