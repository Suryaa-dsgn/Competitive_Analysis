### **10.4 A competitor-displacement sales play**

The strongest first offer is not an unconditional 50% discount. It's a measured comparison.

Proposed offer: Credentialing and Compliance Efficiency Assessment

Invite an eligible healthcare organization to evaluate a representative, appropriately authorized population against its existing process.

Measure:

* Verified source coverage and match resolution  
* Confirmed exclusions or sanctions missed by either process  
* False-positive and false-negative rates against adjudicated ground truth  
* Time to complete initial verification  
* Time to detect updates, by source  
* Analyst hours spent resolving exceptions  
* Evidence completeness and auditability  
* Total projected operating cost

End with a written business case showing whether Quickflows can achieve equal or better quality with lower operating costs.

For a first pilot, I would use a stratified sample that includes different states, specialties, credential types, ambiguous identities, missing information and known edge cases. A 1,000-record sample could be a reasonable starting design, but the final sample size must be sufficient for the claims being tested.

A comparison that shows fewer alerts isn't automatically superior: it could also indicate missed true positives. Every accuracy claim needs independently adjudicated evidence.

Healthcare personal data also requires proper authority, secure handling, agreements where applicable, and controlled access. A publicly accessible NPI is not a substitute for permission to process private customer records.

### **10.5 What the pricing message should be**

Avoid leading with:

> We offer the same product at half the price.

A more compelling proposition would be:

> We help healthcare teams reduce the cost and time of keeping providers and employees verified, with source-backed evidence, continuous monitoring and AI-assisted exception resolution. We can benchmark the accuracy, coverage and operational cost against your current process.

A discount becomes useful when accompanied by a demonstrated cost advantage and appropriate margin. Until then, it should be a negotiable commercial lever, not the whole market position.

### **The deeper issue with primary-source data**

Official data being accessible does not mean it is complete or sufficient for all verification requirements.

For example, CMS makes NPPES data publicly downloadable and queryable, but NPI registration information is not the same as a full verification of professional licensure or eligibility. HHS-OIG similarly provides downloadable exclusion records, while identity confirmation may require additional information not contained in that file.

CMS+1

This is precisely the layer where a strong commercial verification provider can create value.
