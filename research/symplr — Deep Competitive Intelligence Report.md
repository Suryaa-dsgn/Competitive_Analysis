### **3.2 symplr CVO — the most important service competitor**

CVO stands for Credentials Verification Organization.

Here, customers aren't buying only software. They are outsourcing some or all of the credentialing work to an organization with specialized staff, procedures and verification infrastructure.

symplr CVO's documented capabilities include:

* Primary-source verification and supporting evidence  
* Licensure application and management  
* CAQH administration and payer enrollment  
* Delegated credentialing and audit support  
* Sanction and expiration monitoring  
* Credentialing-file preparation and quality review  
* Short-term backlog and surge-capacity support

The service is described as NCQA-certified across all 11 relevant verification areas. symplr also advertises hundreds of credentialing specialists, although different sections of its public website show figures exceeding 500 and 700 respectively. We should verify the definition and latest count before using either as a definitive workforce number.

symplr

A significant finding for Quickflows

symplr directly addresses low-cost automated competitors on its CVO page.

Its message is essentially: automation is useful, but a cheap automated service may miss the active investigation, payer communication and quality review required to produce defensible credentialing files.

This is deliberate competitive positioning—not proof that all automated competitors are inaccurate.

symplr

That tells us how symplr will probably defend itself against Quickflows.

If Quickflows says, "We can automate credentialing for half the price," symplr can respond, "Who investigates incomplete source data, confirms ambiguous records, contacts the payer and guarantees the file is ready for review?"

We must be able to demonstrate the operational answer, not just the AI architecture.

### **3.3 symplr Payer — a different, potentially larger buyer**

Health plans need credentialing data not only to verify eligibility, but also to operate networks, maintain directory information, support audits, track contracts and keep claims-related records accurate.

symplr Payer offers:

* Automated primary-source verification  
* Credential and sanctions monitoring  
* Provider and network record management  
* Compliance alerts and audit reports  
* Provider contracting and enrollment workflows  
* APIs and data exchange with claims/member systems  
* Network accuracy and directory-data support

It specifically markets regulatory support around CMS, NCQA and the No Surprises Act.

symplr Payer

The key distinction is that payers and provider organizations buy related technology for different reasons.

A hospital may care primarily about getting a physician credentialed, privileged and ready to practice. A payer may care about its network's eligibility, data integrity, compliance, and correct member-facing information.

Quickflows should maintain separate messaging and sales materials for these buyer types.

### **3.4 symplr Directory — the data infrastructure layer**

Directory is not merely a public "Find a Doctor" page.

It consolidates provider, facility, group, location and network information and synchronizes that information with systems that consume it.

Think of an organization where five departments keep different versions of the same physician record. Directory aims to make those records consistent through validation, reconciliation and governed updates.

This is a major integration advantage because the provider record becomes a shared enterprise asset, not a credentialing department's isolated spreadsheet.

The documented Yale New Haven deployment is particularly relevant: the health system used symplr Directory, formerly Phynd360, to consolidate provider information across acquired facilities and connect it bidirectionally to an EHR.

symplr

### **3.5 symplr Access — vendor compliance and an additional revenue model**

Access handles an adjacent but distinct problem: whether sales representatives, contractors and other nonemployees should be allowed inside healthcare facilities.

Its product includes background checks, OIG/SAM screening, expiration reminders, mandatory policies, training assignments and real-time facility check-ins.

The website advertises coverage across more than half of U.S. health systems, 24-hour vendor credentialing options, and more than 3 million monthly check-ins. Those are current product-page claims, not independently verified usage statistics.

symplr

This matters because symplr can potentially monetize both sides of the relationship: the healthcare organization needing compliance controls and the vendor representative who needs approved facility access.
